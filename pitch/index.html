Four steps, inside every pull request. The four steps play by themselves; then two clicks open up steps 1 and 2.

One: triage. Every PR is scored by risk, novelty and your proven level. A typo fix passes untouched.

Two: a three-minute viva. You explain your change in your own words. We grade what you say, never your face or voice.

Three: get corrected. A first miss shows what you missed, with fresh questions. Only a repeat miss, or very high risk, reaches a senior.

Four: grow. Gaps stay open until a later change closes them, and every pass makes the next check lighter. So juniors have a reason to learn: understanding earns faster merges, the speed they love.

[click: the score picks the check] A low score gets a few multiple-choice questions; a high score gets the viva. [click: how the score is made] Risk plus novelty, minus what you have already proven.

If asked: demo data is synthetic and scores are labelled Simulated; the GitHub check is the next build step; nothing is measured yet, the pilot measures it.

If asked why people should understand code when AI keeps getting better: someone has to be accountable for what ships, and future seniors come from juniors who can judge code. 80% of executives traced an incident to AI code; only 16% of seniors say juniors fully understand it.

If asked whether explaining is the same as understanding: we ask how it works and why it was built that way, then one follow-up aimed at the weakest part, so a recited summary is meant to break on the follow-up. Harder judgement questions, like spotting the risk in a change, are the next build. Nothing is measured yet; the pilot measures it.

If asked for the Anthropic study: Jan 2026, 52 mostly junior engineers; the AI group scored 50% on a concept quiz against 67% by hand; the biggest gap was debugging, the ability to tell when code is wrong and why; developers who generated code and then asked questions to understand it kept their learning. Small sample, so say it is a signal, not proof.

If asked why this works: explaining in your own words (self-explanation) and being corrected on what you missed (retrieval practice with feedback) are well-established learning effects. Sources: Chi, Bassok, Lewis, Reimann and Glaser, 1989, Cognitive Science 13(2), 145–182 (self-explanation); Roediger and Karpicke, 2006, Psychological Science 17(3), 249–255 (testing effect: 68% vs 54% recall after two days). Note the testing-effect study used free recall, not code, so say it supports the principle, not our product.

If asked about vivaproof.com, also called Viva: it is for schools and checks who wrote an assignment. We work inside the pull request at work, and build skill over time.

Numbers: 84% and 75% (veterans 55%) Info-Tech 2026, 500+ practitioners; 16% BairesDev Q2 2026, n = 1,569; 81% Airwallex × YouGov, Sep 2025, n = 505, finance functions, SMEs 6–249 staff; +91% Faros AI 2025; 80% Sauce Labs 2026, 400 executives; 50% vs 67% Anthropic RCT, Jan 2026, n = 52; 5,320 = ABS June 2025, ANZSIC 7000, 5–199 staff.

If asked about competitors: HackerRank and CodeSignal detect and flag AI use in assessments; ChatGPT Study Mode guides with questions instead of answers; Claude Code's learning mode asks you to write key lines yourself; SlopBlock skips quizzes only for low-risk PRs. None of them ban AI at work, and we don't either.
