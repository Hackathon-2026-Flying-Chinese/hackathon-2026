# FEIT Hackathon 2026 — Judge Q&A

Recorded 29 Sep 2026: our team's questions to a judge (on the judging panel; clearly a different person from the Airwallex speaker). About 4 minutes. Transcribed with Whisper large-v3 after volume boosting. A few passages are too muffled to make out and are marked below.

## Key takeaways
- **The demo is effectively a must** ("kind of a must if that's possible"). A **finished product is not mandatory**. What is essential is everything behind it: the evidence, the research, the thinking, and the logic behind how you got to the end product.
- **The judge cares more about the back end and a working prototype:** show concretely how it works and what code you wrote. The prototype should be user-friendly.
- **A specific solution plus a roadmap:** after we said the Airwallex problem statements felt too abstract and asked whether a more specific solution with a full roadmap is better, the judge said "Correct".
- **Three screening rounds:** tomorrow's pre-screening is an elimination round that looks at the slides. The **final presentation is on day 3**.
- **Pre-screening mainly looks at the slides, but extras count:** if you already have a website or prototype, they take it into account. Most students only have time for the slides.
- **Format is flexible:** some teams make a Canva poster and send it with the slides. Audio and video are OK, but **embed them properly in the slides**, because you have to present them the next day.

## Q&A

**Q: Is a demo not required?**
A: For me, as a member of the judging panel, a demo is pretty much a must, if at all possible. The end product itself is not mandatory if you run out of time. What is needed is the evidence, how you did the research, the thinking behind it and the logic leading to that end product. So: the demo is preferred but not mandated; everything behind it is required.

**Judge: Have you picked a problem statement?** Us: Still discussing. The Airwallex ones feel too abstract and not specific enough.

**Q: So as a judge, do you prefer a more specific solution with a whole roadmap for the question?**
A: Correct. I'm more interested in all the back-end stuff, i.e. a prototype of what can actually be done with it.

**Q: Say we pick PS2 (irregular payments; the judge seemed to mention employers or young workers). We'd show a [roadmap?] of how we analyse the data. Is that what you're looking for?**
A: Yes, correct. You need to show me exactly what code you wrote, i.e. a demo. It should be user-friendly.
> The middle of this exchange (about 01:28–01:56) is too muffled to transcribe reliably.

**Q: So is it a three-page presentation?**
A: That doesn't really matter.

**Q: Are you judging purely on the presentation?**
A: Not really. There are three rounds of screening. We do the pre-screening part, and the presentation on day 3 is the final.

**Q: Is there an elimination round tomorrow? What if we don't pass pre-screening?**
A: Yes, tomorrow. I only judge the slides there. But if you have, say, a website or a prototype, we take that into account too. Usually students don't have time for anything beyond the slides.
> About 03:14–03:20 is unclear (possibly about how much the slides count, or how many there should be).

**Q: What other formats are allowed?**
A: Some students make something in Canva, a poster for example, and send it along with the slides. Audio or video is fine too, but make sure it is well embedded in the slides, because you need to present it the following day.
