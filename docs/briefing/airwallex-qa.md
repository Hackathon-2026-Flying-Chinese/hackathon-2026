# FEIT Hackathon 2026 — Airwallex Q&A (clarifications)

Recorded 29 Sep 2026: our team's Q&A with the Airwallex speaker about PS1 "Getting Good" and PS2 "Irregular income". Transcribed with Whisper large-v3 after volume boosting and noise reduction. The recording starts partway through, so the opening question is missing.

## Key takeaways
- **Both problem statements are deliberately broad.** They won't steer us to one direction. Any idea that fits under the umbrella is fine.
- **Timeline: tomorrow's pre-screening is 3 slides, no code.** It is the first elimination round. Settle the idea and the 3 slides **today**, then start building straight away rather than waiting a day for results. Realistically there are only about 10–12 more hours to build on Thursday.
- **What to focus on:** Who you are solving for (end user), what the solution is, how it works, and how it addresses the problem as it will look in a few years' time. A full app is not expected (a stretch unless AI writes all the code). The hardest part is getting the idea right.
- **Judging (Airwallex is a fintech):** Where the idea could go (vision) plus the technical side: how it works, how it solves the problem, **and whether it can scale beyond Melbourne**.
- **Follow-up questions:** They can't answer email. Mentors will drop in this afternoon and tomorrow. Use first principles: what does it solve, and who does it solve for? If it fits under the problem statement, it's fine.

## Q&A

**(Opening, question not captured) · PS1 Getting Good**
A: The problem statement is deliberately broad; pick whichever part interests you. Core point: AI can already do the first few layers of entry-level work in most industries (trades, nursing etc. are exceptions). Those roles are where newcomers build practical experience and expertise, the foundation for career progression. The question is **how not to lose that**, and how to help people coming through university or any education pathway bridge the gap. Models improve every few weeks, so what will this look like in 5 years? People still need the understanding that the "first three years" gave them. For example, if AI writes a bug that costs millions of dollars a day, the engineer has to know what went wrong, how to review it, how to troubleshoot it and what the fix options are. That ability used to take years to build.

**Q: Analogy check: AI lifts the weights for me at the gym, so I don't build my own muscle. Is that fair?**
A: Yes, that's a fair analogy.

**Q: Is there a specific target user?**
A: No, you choose.

**Q: The problem statements are abstract. Do you expect a mock-up plus an MVP plan by noon tomorrow?**
A: Not feasible in the time. Focus on the idea and concept, the direction and what it solves, and have **3 slides ready for tomorrow's pre-screening**. Then use the next 24 hours to flesh out the whole idea.

**Q: So you want a good plan rather than a very specific solution?**
A: Yes and no. In this workshop, pin down: who you are solving for, what part of the problem statement your idea addresses, who it is for, how it would work, what it does, and how it solves the problem as it will look in a few years.

**Q: Does detailed coding come later?**
A: Yes. Tomorrow's screen (the first elimination point) is 3 slides, no code. Don't wait for the result, because that wastes a whole day. Settle the idea and the 3 slides today, then move as fast as you can. On Thursday there are only about 10–12 more hours.

**Q: PS2: Is the target user the payer (employer), the landlord, or whoever is in charge?**
A: This one was deliberately provocative because it is an enormous problem. Decide which angle you come in from and who the end user is, then build something.

**Q: The spoken briefing focused on underpayment and the visa cap, but the written statement is about income fitting the system. Are the former context, or do you want the solution to address them?**
A: Treat it as a **systems problem**. Almost everything in any country (rent, tax, loans) depends on proving you can afford it, and that relies on stable, regular income. Casual work is the hardest to prove and doesn't fit how income is currently assessed. There are many ways into it, and they don't want to prescribe one.

**Q: So, for example, proving my income so I can apply for a rental or a loan?**
A: Yes, that is one example. Friends of theirs doing freelance work run several side hustles, each bringing in small amounts from different customers. When they apply for a loan or a rental and need to show financial stability, nobody wants to read a hundred different paychecks. That is a very granular example; the problem is much bigger.

**Q: By "financial infrastructure", do you mean a consumer product, or something closer to a standard that other systems can plug into?**
A: Anything. The wording is **intentionally insanely broad**, and whatever your idea is will fit under the "financial infrastructure" umbrella.

**Q: Do you expect an app by the end of today?**
A: Realistically no, unless you already have an AI agent that codes everything. The hardest part is getting the idea right, then working out who it is for, then what the prototype looks like, and then building.

**Q: Do you care more about a great idea or a highly technical solution?**
A: As a fintech, they are more interested in where the idea could go, but they will **judge from a technical standpoint**: how it works, how it solves the problem, and whether it can scale beyond Melbourne.

**Q: Is there an email for follow-up questions?**
A: They can't reply to email in time. They will be around a bit longer today, and mentors will drop in this afternoon and tomorrow. Go back to first principles: what does it solve, and who does it solve for? If it fits under "Getting Good" or irregular income, it's fine.
